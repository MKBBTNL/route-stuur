Chardonnay/Pinot gris/Pinot noir

The first mention of vines in Joigny in 1082. 

The town was built between the forest, the vineyards and the Yonne river:  wood for houses and barrels, vineyards for trade and water for the river transport of wood and wine. 

- The hillsides sheltered from the north wind 
 -face south/southeast,  - on soft limestone soil covered with clay 
- microclimate due to the river.

the cultivation of vines contributed to the town's economic development. 
In 1776, there were 574 hectares of vines. After its destruction by phylloxera at the end of the 19th century, the Pinot gris and Pinot noir grape varieties were replanted. 

Today, some forty hectares produce white wines from the Chardonnay grape and red wines from Pinot noir. The town is also famous for its Vin Gris, produced from the Pinot Gris grape.
